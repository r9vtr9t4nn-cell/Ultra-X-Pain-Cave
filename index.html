Lower-body strength + trunk stability. No running.
Tuesday · Easy aerobic

Thursday · Gym B

Friday · Easy

Saturday · Rest
Recovery, mobility and normal life. No make-up miles.
Sunday · Long trail run
Z1–Z2 by feel and HR; hike steep climbs, practise fuelling and build time on feet. Pace is terrain-dependent.

Nutrition & hydration
Everyday meals · build the base
Breakfast

Porridge with milk, banana and honey; add yoghurt or protein. Eggs on toast and fruit are another option.

Lunch

Rice, pasta, potatoes or a sandwich with chicken, tuna, eggs, beans or tofu. Add fruit and yoghurt. Keep an easy backup lunch available on busy days.

Dinner

A generous carbohydrate portion, a protein source and vegetables. Increase the rice, pasta or potato portion around harder and longer sessions.

Easy extra energy

Your smoothie option works well when appetite or time is limited: milk, oats, banana or other fruit, honey, peanut butter and protein powder. A snack before bed can help if you've under-eaten during the day.

Long-run fuelling practice
Before

Eat a familiar carbohydrate-rich meal 2–3 hours before a key long run if practical — porridge, toast and banana, or cereal. Start hydrated; avoid experimenting with unfamiliar foods.

During

Start early rather than waiting for hunger. Build towards 60–90 g carbohydrate per hour for long ultra-specific sessions only as your gut tolerates it; begin lower (around 30–60 g/hour) and practise progressively. Use a mix of gels, drink and easy-to-carry foods.

After

Have carbohydrate plus protein within the next couple of hours — for example a recovery smoothie, yoghurt and cereal, or a substantial meal. Replace fluids according to thirst and conditions.

Practise the exact products, quantities, timing and carrying system you expect to use on race day. These are training ranges, not targets to force if your stomach is struggling.
Electrolytes · Precision Fuel salt tablets

I've noted that you may use Precision Fuel salt tablets for the 110K. Treat them as one part of your sodium plan, alongside the sodium already in your drink and other products.

  • Check the sodium amount per tablet on the exact packet you buy — products and serving sizes can differ.
  • Practise them on progressively longer runs, not for the first time on race day.
  • Account for heat, duration, sweat rate, clothing and how salty your sweat is. Don't automatically take a tablet at every fixed interval.
  • Avoid stacking tablets, electrolyte drink and salty foods without adding up their sodium contribution.
  • Drink to thirst; extra salt is not a licence to overdrink. If you develop nausea, headache, confusion or feel seriously unwell during an event, stop and seek medical help.

Before setting a per-hour sodium plan, we'll confirm the exact Precision Fuel tablet (sodium per tablet) and your typical sweat / cramp experience.

Choosing your weights: These kilogram figures are conservative starting estimates, not fixed targets. Machine stacks vary. Choose a load that lets you complete every rep with good technique and around 2–3 reps left in reserve. If it feels too easy, increase slightly; if you strain, lose control or feel pain, reduce it. Do not train to failure.
How to progress

For the first two weeks, prioritise learning the movements and finding suitable loads. When you complete all sets and reps comfortably for two sessions, add the smallest increment available (usually 2.5–5 kg on machines or 1–2 kg per dumbbell). If running fatigue builds, reduce to 2 sets per exercise or use lighter loads. No deadlifts or visual guides.
